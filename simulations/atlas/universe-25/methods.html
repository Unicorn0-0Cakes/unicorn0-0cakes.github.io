01 The source

Universe 25 was a real behavioural experiment run by the ethologist John B. Calhoun at the National Institute of Mental Health between 1968 and 1972. A "mouse utopia" was provisioned with unlimited food, water and nesting material, but with a fixed physical space. Despite the abundance, the colony collapsed — not from starvation, but through behavioural change driven by density.

Calhoun described a progression that this simulation reproduces as four named phases:

PhaseWhat characterises it
Exploration & settlementA small population explores; healthy interaction, balanced territory.
Rapid growthExponential increase, social structures form, early crowding signs.
Social breakdownAggression rises, maternal neglect rises, withdrawn "Beautiful Ones" appear, birth rate turns.
CollapseBirths plummet; behaviour does not recover even as density falls.
What this adds to the original

The historical experiment had no predators, no terrain, no water and no resource limit — the constraint was space alone. Predators, vegetation, water bodies, a flowing river, fish and birds are all invented extensions, added so that "what if the constraint had not been space alone" becomes a question you can ask.

02 Rules and equations

This is an agent-based model on a square lattice. There are no population equations: every mouse, cat, bird and fish is an individual with its own age, health, hunger, thirst and traits, and every population curve on the chart is a count of survivors rather than a solved trajectory.

Mental state

Each mouse occupies one of five discrete states, driven by local density and its own traits:

NORMAL · STRESSED · WITHDRAWN · AGGRESSIVE · BEAUTIFUL ONE

Aggressors disrupt social structure; withdrawn "Beautiful Ones" avoid contact, groom, and do not reproduce; neglectful parents abandon offspring. The collapse is produced by these states accumulating, not by a death rate being raised.

Needs and movement

Hunger and thirst rise every tick. Above a seeking threshold an animal travels to a source; above a deprivation threshold it loses health, weakens, and dies. Water is impassable, so lakes act as barriers that shape movement, hunting and escape; animals route around them using a distance-field pathfinder rather than straight-line steering.

Predation

Cats stay dormant until the colony establishes, then stalk the nearest mouse, pounce when adjacent, gain reserve from each kill, breed when well fed and starve when prey run short. Mice sense nearby predators and flee — but in a crowded crush, escape often fails, which ties predation back to the density theme rather than bolting it on.

Cover, and its twist

Only mice can enter vegetation. A mouse inside grass cannot be caught by a cat — but it must break into the open to reach water, where the cats hunt. Good cover therefore lets a colony escape predation and boom, straight into overcrowding collapse. Birds nest in the vegetation, so cover from cats is not cover from above. emergent

Internal tuning

Time is deliberately compressed against a real colony so that a full rise and fall is watchable. The headline constants (js/config.js):

04 What is simplified

  • Behaviour is five discrete labels, not continuous psychology. A real Calhoun observation sheet had far more categories and much less certainty about them.
  • No disease, no parasitism, no thermoregulation. Death arrives through deprivation, predation, age or violence, and through nothing else.
  • Inheritance is trait-level only. Offspring take blended parental traits; there is no genome and no selection acting on one.
  • Space is a lattice. Territory is emergent from occupancy rather than defended explicitly, and there are no nest sites as physical objects.
  • Time is compressed. The tick is not a day, and generations are not calibrated to mouse biology — they are set so a full run is watchable.
  • The predator–prey balance was tuned for reachability. Coexistence being achievable is a design decision, not a discovery.

A note in the repository records that the original Python/pygame version was incomplete — visualizer.py was missing its update() and draw() methods and crashed on launch. The browser version is the working one; the Python files are kept for reference only.

05 What this cannot establish

  • It is not evidence about mice, and still less about people. Calhoun's experiment became a metaphor for urban overpopulation and social alienation; this model inherits the metaphor, not the standing to test it.
  • It cannot show that density causes behavioural collapse, because the causal link is written into the state-transition rules rather than discovered by them.
  • It cannot tell you the real carrying capacity of anything. The capacities here follow from lattice size and tuned rates.
  • It cannot support a claim about predation policy. That predators can hold a colony below the behavioural-sink threshold is a property of this parameterisation; the model was tuned until coexistence was reachable.
  • It reports no uncertainty. There is no seeding control in the interface, so two runs of the same configuration differ and neither is a replicate of the other.

06 Validation status

No quantitative calibration

Unlike the flask and biosphere models, this one has no benchmark table. Its parameters were set by headless testing until the qualitative behaviour matched the described phase progression and until predator–prey coexistence was reachable. That is tuning, not calibration, and results should be read accordingly.

What was checked

  • Phase progression. A default run reaches Growth → Breakdown → Collapse without the phases being scripted; they are detected from population and state statistics.
  • Predator–prey balance. Headless runs confirm all three regimes are reachable: prey extinction at high predator load, coexistence in a middle band, crowding collapse with no predators.
  • Resource coupling. In finite mode without predators the colony overshoots, strips vegetation, drinks the river down and collapses; with predation holding numbers below carrying capacity the river stays full. emergent
