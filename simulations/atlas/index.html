03 How to read a model

Four habits that make any of these worth more than five minutes of watching dots move.

01

Run it once and don't touch anything

Every instrument opens on a sensible default and starts immediately. Let it play through before you reach for a control — you need a baseline to have an opinion about.

02

Change one thing

Move a single parameter, reset, run again. Changing three at once produces a different world but no information about which change caused what.

03

Watch the mechanism, not the outcome

Say out loud what you expect, then look at how the model got where it got. The value is concentrated almost entirely in the gap between the prediction and the route.

04

Read the limitations

Every instrument has a methods page stating its assumptions, its calibration and what it deliberately does not model. That is where the honest limits are, and it is short.

04 Scientific standard

Not every entry here has the same evidentiary standing, and pretending otherwise would be the quickest way to make the whole catalogue untrustworthy. Each instrument carries a badge.

Sources

Where a simulation is built on documented work — Calhoun's density study, the 1991–93 Biosphere 2 closure, the long-term E. coli evolution experiment, the public-goods literature — the methods page names it and says where the model departs from the record.

Assumptions

Numbers are labelled by provenance: documented, estimated, invented, emergent. Two of the instruments carry a full assumption register; all of them state what they simplify.

Validation

Calibration is reported as a table of model output against published values, including where the model misses. Conservation laws are checked numerically where they apply — Biosphere's carbon ledger closes to within 0.03 per cent over a simulated year.

Reproducibility

Seeded runs replay exactly. Where an instrument reports a sensitivity ranking, decoy parameters are wired into the screen and read by nothing, so the noise floor is measured rather than assumed.

A result that survives a decoy check is worth more than a result that merely agrees with you.
