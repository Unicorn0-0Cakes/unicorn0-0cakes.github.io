01 The source

Three papers, all from Rutherford's laboratory at Manchester.

  • Geiger, H. and Marsden, E. (1909). "On a Diffuse Reflection of the α-Particles." Proceedings of the Royal Society A 82, 495–500. Received 19 May, read 17 June 1909.
  • Rutherford, E. (1911). "The Scattering of α and β Particles by Matter and the Structure of the Atom." Philosophical Magazine, series 6, 21(125), 669–688. Written at the University of Manchester, April 1911; a brief account had been given to the Manchester Literary and Philosophical Society in February.
  • Geiger, H. and Marsden, E. (1913). "The Laws of Deflexion of α Particles through Large Angles." Philosophical Magazine, series 6, 25(148), 604–623. The quantitative test of the 1911 theory, and the source of every number in this instrument that has a number attached to it.

What happened

In 1909 Geiger and Marsden put a tube of radium emanation behind a lead plate, arranged a zinc-sulphide screen so that no α particle could reach it directly, and set a metal reflector in front of the tube. Scintillations appeared at once. They counted them for eight metals and found the rate rising with atomic weight — 3.4 a minute for aluminium against 67 for gold, a ratio of about twenty, where for β particles the same ratio is about two. Varying the number of gold foils showed the effect was a volume effect and not a surface one. Finally, using radium C deposited on a plate and a platinum reflector of about a square centimetre, they estimated the fraction: of the incident α particles, about 1 in 8,000 was reflected.

Their own comment is the point of the whole thing:

"If the high velocity and mass of the α-particle be taken into account, it seems
surprising that some of the α-particles, as the experiment shows, can be turned
within a layer of 6 × 10⁻⁵ cm. of gold through an angle of 90°, and even more. To
produce a similar effect by a magnetic field, the enormous field of 10⁹ absolute
units would be required."
                            — Geiger and Marsden 1909, p. 498

In 1911 Rutherford showed that a multitude of small deflections could not account for it. On Thomson's atom the chance of a 90° deflection is, in his phrase, "vanishingly small"; the observed rate was not. He proposed instead a charge concentrated at the centre of the atom, worked out the hyperbolic orbit of a particle passing near it, and derived the distribution that the scattered particles must follow.

In 1913 Geiger and Marsden tested that distribution. Over several weeks they counted more than a hundred thousand scintillations at angles from 5° to 150°, across which the number of scattered particles varied by a factor of about 250,000, and found the predicted 1/sin⁴(φ/2) law held. They also confirmed the proportionality to foil thickness, the dependence on the square of the atomic weight, and the inverse fourth power of the velocity — and made one absolute measurement, from which they deduced that "the number of elementary charges composing the centre of the atom is equal to half the atomic weight."

That last conclusion is wrong, and instructively so. Half the atomic weight of gold is 98.5; the nuclear charge is 79. The instrument uses the modern value and section 06 shows what the difference costs.

What this instrument is

A historical reconstruction using a classical computational model. The two competing angular distributions are implemented as Rutherford set them out, side by side, in the 1911 paper. The apparatus is schematic and the exposures are counted in particles rather than in weeks. It is not a reconstruction of the apparatus, and the catalogue's evidence field is left unset until the validation in section 06 is extended — see the note there.

02 Rules and equations

The nuclear model

An α particle of charge Z₁e and kinetic energy E passes an atom whose central charge is Z₂e. Rutherford's §2 gives the head-on distance of closest approach, the point at which all the kinetic energy has become potential energy:

b = Z₁ Z₂ k e² / E

and the deflection in terms of the impact parameter p:

cot(φ/2) = 2p / b

His §3 turns this into a distribution. For a beam falling on a foil of thickness t containing n atoms per unit volume, the fraction deflected through more than φ is his equation (3),

P(> φ) = (π/4) · n · t · b² · cot²(φ/2)

and the number arriving per unit area of screen is his equation (5), which in modern notation is a density per unit solid angle:

dP/dΩ = n · t · (b/4)² · cosec⁴(φ/2)

From these he reads off the four dependences that Geiger and Marsden went on to check: the scattering goes as cosec⁴(φ/2), as the thickness t, as the square of the central charge, and as the inverse fourth power of the velocity.

How the model samples it

The impact parameter is taken uniformly in area over the projected area per atom in the foil,

p_max = 1 / √(π n t) ,   p = p_max √u ,   u ~ U(0,1)
φ = 2 · arctan( b / 2p )

which reproduces equation (3) exactly. This is worth stating plainly because it also solves a numerical problem: the Rutherford cross-section diverges as φ → 0, and bounding p at p_max cuts the distribution off at a smallest possible deflection

φ_min = 2 · arctan( b / 2 p_max )

below which no particle emerges. The distribution is therefore normalised on [φ_min, π] by construction. There is no divergence to regularise by hand, no probability greater than one, and no arbitrary cut-off angle. For the reference gold foil φ_min is 0.335°.

In the real world that cut-off comes from screening by the atomic electrons instead. The two mechanisms are different and reach a similar place; this is noted again in section 04.

The diffuse model

Rutherford's §5 states Thomson's compound-scattering result in the form that makes the two theories comparable. The average deflection from a single atom of radius R with charge N e distributed through it is π b_T / 8R; after a thickness t the particle has passed through π R² n t atoms, and the deflections add in quadrature, so the characteristic angle is

θ_t = (π b_T / 8R) · √(π R² n t) = (π b_T / 8) · √(π n t)

— R cancels, which is convenient, because no atomic radius has to be invented. Rutherford then gives the survival function for compound scattering directly:

P(> φ) = exp( −φ² / θ_t² )

This is the whole implementation. It is not a ray-trace through a Thomson atom; it is the published law, sampled by inverting it.

The charge in the diffuse model

b_T is built from Thomson's charge, not Rutherford's. On the diffuse picture the deflecting charge is the atom's whole complement of corpuscles, and Crowther had deduced from β-ray scattering "that the number of electrons in an atom was about three times its atomic weight" (Rutherford 1911, §1). The model uses N_T = 3A — 591 for gold.

This matters. A Thomson atom given N = Z would fail at both ends and the comparison would be a straw man. With N_T = 3A the diffuse model reproduces roughly the small-angle scattering that was actually observed in 1910 — at 1° it predicts more scattering than the nuclear model does — and still predicts nothing whatever past twenty degrees. That is the historical situation, and it is why the experiment had to be done at large angles.

The detector

The detector is a circular aperture of angular radius ρ whose axis makes an angle φ_d with the beam. Its solid angle is Ω = 2π(1 − cos ρ), and the acceptance is the integral of the exit density over that cap.

The integral is done over the polar angle rather than over the cap, because the density can vary by ten orders of magnitude inside a wide aperture and no fixed rule on the cap will resolve a forward peak a third of a degree across. Integrating over φ instead — where the structure is, and where the model says exactly where it is — leaves the azimuthal part in closed form. A point on the ring at polar angle φ and azimuth ψ, measured from the plane through the beam and the detector axis, is inside the cap when

cos φ cos φ_d + sin φ sin φ_d cos ψ ≥ cos ρ

so the fraction of that ring inside the cap is arccos(X)/π with X = (cos ρ − cos φ cos φ_d)/(sin φ sin φ_d), clipped to all or none. Only φ in [φ_d − ρ, φ_d + ρ] contributes at all, which bounds the range, and φ_min and θ_t are inserted as breakpoints inside it. The result agrees with the analytic P(>φ) to eight figures whenever the two can be compared (section 06).

Where randomness enters

Three places, and nowhere else:

StageDistributionWhy
Particles reaching the apertureBinomial(N, acceptance) Each of N particles independently either lands in the cap or does not.
Particles the screen recordsBinomial(reached, ε) Nested inside the first, which is what makes detected ≤ eligible ≤ fired true by construction rather than by a clamp after the fact.
Background countsPoisson(rate × N / 10⁹) Stray particles off the diaphragm and the vessel walls, proportional to beam intensity.

The acceptance itself is computed by quadrature rather than by simulating particles one at a time. An exposure of 10⁹ therefore costs the same as one of 10³, and the counting statistics are exact rather than approximated by a small sample. The trajectories drawn on the apparatus view are a separate subsample from the same laws, used for illustration only; no count anywhere derives from them.

The generator is Mulberry32. A session has a seed; exposure k derives its own generator from (seed, k), so the same seed and the same sequence of actions reproduce a run exactly, while two exposures at identical settings are still independent counts. In blind mode the hidden model is chosen from the seed as well, so a whole session including its answer is reproducible.

Numerical guards

  • Every value leaving the model passes a finiteness check with a fallback; nothing that is not a number reaches a screen or an export.
  • Probabilities are clamped to [0,1] and the exit distribution is checked against unity — the analytic path is normalised by construction, the beam-convolved table integrates to within 0.2 per cent and is renormalised.
  • Angles are clamped to [0, π] at every entry and exit. cos values into arccos are clamped to [−1, 1].
  • Counts are integers, non-negative, and bounded above by the stage before them.
  • Energy is clamped to [0.1, 60] MeV, Z to [1, 120], thickness to [0.1, 10⁶] nm, before any arithmetic touches them.
  • The 0/0 case that would produce a NaN — impact parameter exactly zero — instead produces arctan(∞) = π/2 and a deflection of exactly π, which is the physically correct answer.

What still needs a decision

Three of the pedagogical values above — the background rate, the beam spread and the exposure scale — have no counterpart in the papers and were chosen for playability. They set how ambiguous a small exposure feels, which is a teaching choice rather than a physical one, and they are the obvious things to argue about. Changing any of them changes the difficulty of the instrument and none of its physics.

04 What is simplified

Multiple scattering is not implemented

Every deflection is a single encounter. This is exactly the regime Rutherford's theory was derived for — he notes that if the probability of one deflection φ is 1/1000, the probability of two is 1/10⁶ and "is negligibly small" — and it holds comfortably for the reference foil, where 0.45 per cent of particles are deflected past 5°.

It stops holding for thick foils. The instrument monitors P(>5°) and labels the run strained above 5 per cent and failing above 20 per cent, on the apparatus screen and in the exported configuration. The 4000 nm setting is past the failure threshold and is offered anyway, because watching a model announce its own breakdown is more useful than being prevented from reaching it. In that regime the small-angle end of the distribution is understated; counts past about 30° are still trustworthy, because a large deflection is still overwhelmingly likely to have been a single encounter. Geiger and Marsden found the scattering strictly proportional to thickness for thin foils and rising somewhat faster for thicker ones; that excess is absent here.

Energy loss in the foil

The beam leaves the foil at the energy it entered with. Geiger and Marsden applied a correction for this, "amounted to 9 per cent in this experiment for the thickest foil used", and it matters because the scattering goes as 1/E². For the reference foil it is negligible; at 4000 nm it is not, and it compounds with the multiple-scattering failure above.

Nuclear recoil

The scattering centre is treated as infinitely heavy. Rutherford calculates the real effect in his §4: an α deflected 90° by gold loses about 2 per cent of its velocity, but by aluminium it loses 14 per cent, and "the general theory of single scattering will require modification" when the target mass approaches the α mass. The light targets offered here — aluminium and carbon — are therefore the least accurate, and carbon is the worst.

Electron screening

The atomic electrons are absent. Rutherford shows their contribution to large deflections is negligible for heavy atoms, which justifies leaving them out of the nuclear model's mechanism. But they are also what cuts off the divergence at very small angles in reality, and here that cut-off comes instead from the finite projected area per atom. Two different mechanisms reaching a similar place: the smallest deflections in this instrument are right in magnitude and wrong in origin.

Trajectories versus scintillations

The apparatus view draws particle paths. Nothing of the sort was ever visible. What Geiger and Marsden saw, after half an hour in a dark room getting their eyes in, was a faint flash on a zinc-sulphide screen, one particle at a time, counted through a microscope by a person who could not manage "more than 90 scintillations per minute or less than about 5". The paths on screen are drawn from the same laws that produce the counts, but they are an illustration of the model's working and the view labels them as such. No number in the ledger comes from them.

The apparatus itself

Schematic. The 1913 chamber was a brass ring 3.5 cm deep with internal and external diameters of 5.5 and 7.5 cm, evacuated with charcoal cooled by liquid air, with the source 2.5 cm from the foil and the screen moving on a circle of 1.6 cm radius. None of that is reproduced, and none of it would change a count in this model, which depends on the foil only through n·t and on the detector only through its angle and its solid angle.

No quantum mechanics anywhere

The model is entirely classical: a hyperbolic orbit in an inverse-square field. The Rutherford cross-section happens to survive the transition to quantum theory for pure Coulomb scattering — the Born approximation and the exact non-relativistic quantum treatment both return the same formula — which is a famous accident and not a general licence. Nothing else in the classical picture survives. There is also no nuclear force: at high enough energy the α reaches the nucleus and Coulomb's law stops describing the encounter, and that limit is neither modelled nor flagged.

05 What this cannot establish

  • The quantum structure of the atom. Rutherford's nucleus is a point charge in a classical orbit problem. It says nothing about energy levels or spectra, and the atom it implies is unstable — an orbiting electron radiates. Rutherford knew this and set it aside explicitly: "The question of the stability of the atom proposed need not be considered at this stage."
  • Electron orbitals. There are no electrons in this model at all, only a compensating charge that has been shown to be negligible for the deflections of interest. Nothing about the arrangement of the outer charge can be inferred from anything the instrument produces.
  • Nuclear substructure. The nucleus here has a charge and no size, no constituents, no excited states and no strong force. It is a point, and the model breaks down exactly where a real nucleus would start to be interesting.
  • The sign of the central charge. Rutherford's own conclusion, and this instrument inherits it: "The deductions from the theory so far considered are independent of the sign of the central charge, and it has not so far been found possible to obtain definite evidence to determine whether it be positive or negative."
  • An exact reconstruction of the historical experiment. The geometry is schematic, the exposure is measured in particles rather than in weeks of counting, the background is a pedagogical number, and several apparatus dimensions are not reproduced. Counterfactuals run in the instrument are statements about this model, not about what Geiger and Marsden would have found.
  • The nuclear charge, from your own data. You can measure a distribution consistent with a concentrated charge. Extracting Z from it requires the absolute normalisation, which this instrument gives you rather than asking you to measure — so the agreement in section 06 is a check on the arithmetic, not a result you derived.
  • That the diffuse-charge model is impossible. What the instrument shows is that a particular diffuse model, with a particular charge, predicts nothing at large angles. A count at 120° rules that model out. It does not rule out every conceivable distribution of charge, and no finite set of exposures could.

Internal consistency

  • Normalisation. Across six configurations × two models, the exit distribution integrated over the whole sphere differs from one by at most 2 × 10⁻³. Every P(>φ) sampled at 5° intervals is in [0,1] and finite; every density is non-negative and finite.
  • Quadrature. The cap integral at φ_d = 0 agrees with the analytic P(>ρ) to eight significant figures for ρ from 1° to 90°. The acceptance summed over a partition of the sphere into 10° annuli comes to 1 within 3 × 10⁻³.
  • Determinism. The same seed and the same sequence of exposures reproduce identical observations; a different seed does not. Two exposures at identical settings within a session draw independently. The hidden model in blind mode is a function of the seed alone, and across 400 seeds both models are chosen a comparable number of times.
  • Accounting. Across every exposure taken in the suite, detected ≤ eligible ≤ fired, and raw = detected + background, with no exceptions. Ledger totals match the sum of the exposures.
  • Numerical integrity. Nine deliberately awkward configurations — detector at 0° and at 180°, the widest and narrowest apertures, the thinnest and thickest foils, Z overridden to 1, 10¹⁰ particles at full efficiency with maximum background — produce no NaN, no infinity, no out-of-range probability and no impossible count, in either model.
  • Counting statistics. Over 400 exposures the mean count matches the model's prediction within four standard errors and the variance matches the mean, as Poisson counting requires.
  • Rare events stay rare. A small exposure at 150° returns nothing at all in a substantial fraction of trials but not in all of them. The diffuse model returns zero counts at 60° in 100 trials of 10¹⁰ particles each.
  • Exports match the screen. The observation CSV is compared field by field against the ledger objects it was built from — angle, raw count, corrected count and seed on every row. The model-comparison CSV is compared against the comparison table. Blind mode is checked not to leak the hidden model into either.
  • Reset. A new session starts empty and leaves the old one untouched.

Interface and accessibility, checked in the suite

These were tested against the source and against a stubbed DOM, not by a person looking at a screen — see the caveat below.

  • Theming. Every --rf-* token the canvases read is defined in both the day and the night block of orbital.css, and so is every token the page stylesheet maps. No colour is hard-coded anywhere outside the print rule. Every canvas repaints under both themes without error.
  • Reduced motion. Detected from the media query and from the chrome bar's Pause class; in that state the animation step is a no-op, the paths are drawn statically, and the view says so. Counts are byte-identical whether the animation is running or not. Scintillation marks fade over 900 ms with a 400 ms minimum gap, so the view cannot exceed 2.5 transitions a second.
  • Keyboard. Every shortcut is driven through the real handler: exposure, sweep, detector angle and aperture with their bounds, all seven screens, reset, help, Escape. Shortcuts do not fire on the mode chooser, while a dialogue is open, while a field has focus, or with a modifier held. Every control the screens emit is a button, input, select or a — nothing is clickable without being focusable.
  • Layout. No fixed width above 340 px anywhere in the stylesheet; breakpoints at 1080 px and 720 px; tables scroll inside their own container; charts size from the element rather than a constant; a print stylesheet exists.
  • Charts have text. Every canvas carries an aria-label, and every chart has a written summary element that is non-empty after painting. The distribution plot also offers the same data as a table.
  • Offline. No fetch, no XMLHttpRequest, no CDN, no localStorage, and no URL outside the Google Fonts stylesheet in the head.
  • Links. Every src and href in both pages resolves on disk; every element id the scripts ask for exists; every data-screen has a section. The badges on this page are read from assets/catalogue.js rather than written here, and the catalogue record's taxonomy strings are checked against TAXONOMY.
Not yet checked by a person in a browser

Everything above was verified headlessly. Nobody has yet opened this instrument in a real browser, watched the animation, tabbed through it with a screen reader, or looked at it on a phone. The tests establish that the mechanisms are wired correctly; they cannot establish that the result is legible, that the night theme has enough contrast where it matters, or that the apparatus view reads as an apparatus. That pass is outstanding.

Why the evidence badge is not set

The catalogue's evidence field is null, which renders as a dashed "Status pending" chip. On the site's own definitions this instrument is a candidate for Historical reconstruction, and the checks above are more than most things carry. What is missing is a comparison against the full 1913 dataset rather than the single absolute point — the angular series for all six metals, the thickness series and the velocity series, each fitted and residuals reported, with the arbitrary scale factors of each block handled properly. Until that table exists in this section, the badge would be asserting more than has been shown.

Two known discrepancies belong in it when it is written. The 1913 velocity scale runs about 7 per cent high against modern α energies, which propagates to about 30 per cent in b². And their Z = A/2 is 25 per cent above the true nuclear charge for gold, which is a factor of 1.55 in the predicted rate. Both are understood; neither has been quantified across the whole dataset.

07 Version history

First working version. Both scattering laws implemented from Rutherford 1911 — the nuclear model from §§2–3 by sampling the impact parameter uniformly in area, the diffuse model from §5 by inverting the compound-scattering survival function with Crowther's N_T = 3A. Detector acceptance by closed-form azimuthal integration over a polar quadrature. Four modes: guided reconstruction, blind identification, model comparison, free laboratory. Angular histogram, polar plot, detector sweep, model overlays and evidence ledger.
