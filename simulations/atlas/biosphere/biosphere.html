Biosphere:
Closed World

Eight people, seven biomes, one atmosphere and a great deal of soil, sealed inside three acres of glass. Nothing leaves. Nothing disappears. Everything goes somewhere — and finding out where is the entire game.

You are the mission systems director. Before the airlock closes you decide almost everything. Afterwards you diagnose a living machine through incomplete instruments, and intervene without breaking something you cannot see.

Space to pause · 1–5 for speed
On the history. The first Biosphere 2 closure began on 26 September 1991 with eight participants and ran for two years. Oxygen fell from a normal atmospheric level to roughly 14 per cent over about sixteen months, while carbon dioxide conspicuously failed to rise in proportion. Published work attributes the decline largely to microbial respiration in carbon-rich soils, with concrete carbonation absorbing much of the carbon dioxide that respiration produced. This simulation is historically informed. It is not a reconstruction, its crew are fictional, and every screen labels which of its numbers are documented, which are estimated, and which were invented so that the thing could be played.
