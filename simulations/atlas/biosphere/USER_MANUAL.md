# Biosphere: Closed World — player's manual

> Nothing leaves. Nothing disappears. Everything goes somewhere.

You are the mission systems director. Before the airlock closes you decide almost everything. Afterwards you
diagnose a living machine through imperfect instruments and intervene without breaking something you cannot see.

---

## Your first hour

1. Choose **Guided mission — 365 days** on the home screen.
2. Read the closure card, then press **Begin at sunrise**.
3. Leave the speed at 4× and watch the **Command** screen for a few simulated weeks.
4. When the first alert appears, do not act on it. Open **Atmosphere** and look at the four-day chart first.

The single most useful habit: before changing anything, find out whether you are looking at a level or a rate.

---

## Reading the world

**The mission bar** carries only the values that would make you stop what you are doing. Every one shows a
direction and, where it matters, a rate. `Oxygen 18.7 % ↓ 0.03/day` is a very different statement from
`Oxygen 18.7 %`.

**The cutaway** is the whole enclosure in section. Canopy height, soil colour, condensation on the glass,
rain, crop rows, the basement machinery and its indicator lights are all live. Six overlays turn the same
drawing into a different diagram:

| Overlay | Shows |
| --- | --- |
| Airflow | How hard the air handlers are mixing biomes |
| Water | Soil moisture against each biome's design target, with evaporation rising |
| Heat | Temperature against setpoint — red above, blue below |
| Carbon | Net kg C per day per biome. Green fixes more than it respires |
| Labour | Where the crew actually are, and whether demand exceeds hours |
| Species | Invasive pressure, biome by biome |

**The inspector** on the right answers, for whatever you selected: what is this, what is happening, why might
it be happening, what can you do, and what else could that affect.

**The bottom rail** shows oxygen hour by hour across the last four days, with night shaded, plus what is coming.

## The seven levers that actually matter

1. **Ration.** Full, reduced, or emergency. It is a crew decision and it is logged as one.
2. **Irrigation, per biome.** The farm sits under its own vault and receives almost no rain, so its water is
   whatever you give it. Wetter soil also means faster microbial respiration.
3. **Labour priorities.** You set what matters, not who stands where. Demand almost always exceeds hours.
4. **Climate control effort.** Cooling and heating both cost power. Soil respiration roughly doubles for every
   ten degrees, so the chillers are quietly an atmospheric control.
5. **Air mixing.** High mixing evens out temperature and moves moisture into biomes that were not designed for it.
6. **Supplemental light and the scrubber.** Light adds photosynthesis at 18 kW a bank. The scrubber removes
   carbon dioxide without touching oxygen — worth knowing when those two problems come apart.
7. **Preventive maintenance.** The first thing sacrificed and the most expensive thing to have sacrificed.

## The loop

**Observe** conditions, alerts and trends. **Investigate** on the system screens and by comparing two dates.
**Form a hypothesis** in the science workbench. **Design a test** — some of them are interventions and change
the world when you begin them. **Advance time.** **Review** the prediction against the result.

Experiments end in one of three ways: supported, not supported, or **inconclusive**, which happens when two
variables moved together and three weeks was not enough to separate them. All three go in the notebook. The
mission is scored on explanation, not only on survival.

---

## Alerts

| Level | Meaning |
| --- | --- |
| Advisory | Nothing to do. Worth knowing. |
| Watch | A trend that may become dangerous. |
| Action required | Assign labour or make a decision. |
| Emergency | The clock stops. |

Every alert states what was detected, why it matters, the evidence, the measurement confidence, and what you
might investigate. None of them tell you the cause. Use the button in the mission bar to choose which levels
are allowed to stop the clock.

## Things worth knowing before they happen

**Oxygen and carbon dioxide are not the same problem.** Oxygen can fall for a year while carbon dioxide barely
moves, because something other than the biology is absorbing the carbon. Finding that sink is the first
campaign. Sealing it in Architect mode is instructive and extremely uncomfortable.

**Leakage is a red herring.** The envelope loses every gas in proportion, so it moves pressure and leaves
composition almost untouched. Test it anyway — a refuted hypothesis honestly recorded is worth more than an
unexamined one that happened to be right.

**The farm will not quite feed you.** It delivers roughly three-quarters to four-fifths of the calorie
requirement, which is close to what the historical crew experienced. The gap closes with rationing, planting
choices, or the imported reserve — and the reserve is recorded as a break in food closure.

**Protein and variety bind before calories do.** Beans and peanuts take three months. Planting them next week
is not the same as planting them today.

**Injecting oxygen is not cheating.** It is an intervention with a cost, and the final report records the
amount, the day, what the atmosphere was doing either side of it, and what it bought. What it does not do is
change the cause.

**Thin air is a labour problem long before it is a medical one.** Below about 17 per cent everything simply
takes longer, so maintenance slips, so machines fail, so more work appears.

**The ants are not a bug.** An introduced tramp species reorganising the invertebrate community is what
happened, and the pesticide option puts a residue into an atmosphere, a water cycle and a food supply that
have nowhere to send it.

## Endings

There is no *Victory* or *Failure*. There is a classification:

- Sustainable closed system
- Technically sustained system
- Biologically stable but socially fractured
- Human survival with ecological collapse
- Ecological survival with mission evacuation
- Scientifically valuable failure
- Unexplained survival
- Cascading systems collapse

Surviving the year on constant imports without understanding anything gets a different ending from working out
the mechanism. That is deliberate.

---

## Counterfactuals worth running in Architect mode

- Lean low-carbon soil instead of rich soil. Watch what happens to the oxygen curve.
- Seal the concrete before closure. Watch what happens to carbon dioxide, and to nothing else.
- A far larger farm and a much smaller rainforest.
- Ten crops of one thing versus a spread of six.
- Strict closure with no reserves at all.

Whatever you find is a property of this model. It is not a discovery about the real facility, and the report
will remind you of that.
