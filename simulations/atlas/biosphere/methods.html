01 The source

The first closure of Biosphere 2 ran from September 1991 to September 1993: eight people sealed inside roughly three acres of glass in Arizona, with seven biomes, an ocean, an agricultural system and a great deal of soil. The first campaign here is built around the documented atmospheric anomaly of that closure.

The anomaly the model is about

Oxygen fell from a normal atmospheric level to roughly 14 per cent over about sixteen months, while carbon dioxide conspicuously failed to rise in proportion. Carbon was going somewhere other than the air. Finding out where is the entire mechanic.

Historical position

  • The crew are fictional and are not portrayals of the historical participants.
  • Every screen labels its numbers as historical estimated or model.
  • The in-page Assumptions screen separates what is documented, what is derived from published ranges, what was invented for playability, and what is not represented at all.
  • A counterfactual result is a statement about this model. It is never presented as evidence about the real facility, and the end-of-mission report says so explicitly.

02 Rules and equations

This is a stock-and-flow systems model integrated on a daily step, not an agent model. The eight crew are individuals; everything else is a stock with rates in and out.

SystemWhat it models
AtmosphereO₂, CO₂, pressure, humidity, diurnal and seasonal gas exchange, envelope leakage
CarbonPhotosynthesis, plant and soil respiration, litter, humification, concrete carbonation, ocean dissolution
WaterEvapotranspiration, condensation, rainfall distribution, irrigation, treatment, potable and grey streams
AgricultureTwelve crops with real growth curves, nutrition, labour, seed stocks, pests, storage life
EcologyFunctional roles rather than individuals — pollinators, decomposers, herbivores, an invasive tramp ant, a reef
CrewEight people with health, fatigue, morale, body mass, skills, sleep debt and opinions
TechnosphereTen machines with condition, wear driven by humidity and heat, failure, spares and repair skill
ClosureSeparate ledgers for atmospheric, water, nutrient, food, biological, mechanical and informational closure

Everything is coupled

Human breath becomes plant carbon; plant waste becomes soil; soil microbes change the air; rainforest humidity becomes condensation; condensation becomes stored water; food shortages reduce labour; delayed labour damages the machinery that keeps the food growing. There is no isolated subsystem to optimise.

Diagnosis, not accumulation

The interface does not tell you what is wrong. It shows evidence:

Oxygen concentration has declined for 31 consecutive days. Carbon dioxide is not rising proportionally. Night-time oxygen loss exceeds daytime recovery. Soil temperature in the agricultural biome has risen 1.7 °C.

The hypothesis workbench exists so you can commit to an explanation before the model confirms or refuses it.

03 Parameters

Modes

  • Guided mission (365 days) — a facility built to the historical specification. The recommended first run.
  • Architect mode — a six-stage design wizard: purpose, biome areas, soil carbon, concrete sealing, pollinator strategy, crew selection from a pool of twelve, reserves against a fixed budget, and the closure protocol that defines what counts as intervention.
  • Short mission (120 days) — one full crop cycle, a single sitting.
  • No outside help (365 days) — strict closure. No oxygen, food, parts or outside expertise may enter.

The high-leverage design parameters

ParameterWhy it matters
Soil organic carbonThe dominant term in the oxygen budget. Lean soil ends a year nearly two percentage points higher in O₂.
Concrete sealingUnsealed concrete carbonates, absorbing CO₂ and hiding the carbon. Sealing it before closure raises CO₂ roughly fivefold while barely touching the oxygen curve.
Biome areasSets photosynthetic capacity against respiratory load.
Pollinator strategyDetermines whether the crop system reproduces without hand labour.
Crew compositionSkill coverage against the ten machines; a repair gap compounds.
Closure protocolDefines what counts as intervention, and therefore what a "successful" run means.

Constants, the biome table, crop table, crew pool, machinery and the calibration handles are in js/config.js. The world itself is js/model.js, which is deliberately free of DOM access so it can be driven from node.

04 What is simplified

  • Ecology is functional, not taxonomic. Pollinators, decomposers and herbivores are roles with rates, not species with populations. One invasive tramp ant and one reef are represented specifically; nothing else is.
  • The daily step hides sub-daily dynamics. Diurnal gas exchange is modelled as a within-day term rather than integrated at finer resolution.
  • Soil is one pool per biome, with humification as a single transfer, rather than a layered profile with distinct microbial guilds.
  • Crew psychology is four scalars — health, fatigue, morale, sleep debt — plus opinions. The documented interpersonal history of the real closure is not modelled and deliberately so.
  • Machinery is ten items with a condition scalar, not a maintenance graph.
  • Weather is seasonal forcing, not a simulated climate.

05 What this cannot establish

  • It cannot tell you what actually happened in Biosphere 2. It reproduces a documented trajectory from an assumed mechanism; agreement with the record is not proof of the mechanism.
  • It cannot apportion the real oxygen loss between soil respiration and concrete carbonation. The split here follows from parameters you set.
  • It cannot support engineering claims about closed life-support systems. Nothing here is dimensioned against a real facility other than the one it is loosely modelled on.
  • It cannot say anything about the people involved. The crew are invented, and a bad outcome in a run is a property of the model's rates, not a judgement about anyone.
  • It cannot be used to argue that a counterfactual design would have worked. A counterfactual result is a statement about this model, and the end-of-mission report says so on the page.

06 Validation status

js/model.js is free of DOM access so it can be driven headlessly. Three checks were run.

1 · Carbon conservation

Total carbon across soil, litter, biomass, food, bodies, atmospheric CO₂, carbonated concrete and ocean DIC closes to within 0.03 per cent over 365 simulated days.

What the check caught

Three separate carbon-creating bugs were found this way — humification returning more carbon than it moved, herbivores respiring carbon they had not eaten, and crew exhaling carbon that was never taken out of the food store. A conservation test is worth more than a plausible-looking output curve.

2 · Historical pace

A control run ends 365 days at 16.6 per cent oxygen, on the documented trajectory.

3 · Counterfactual behaviour

Lean soil ends nearly two percentage points higher in oxygen. Sealing the concrete before closure raises CO₂ roughly fivefold while leaving the oxygen curve almost untouched — which is the entire lesson of the scenario, and it is arrived at by the model rather than scripted. emergent

07 Version history

First public release. Four modes, eight coupled systems, the cutaway with six diagnostic overlays, the hypothesis workbench, and the end-of-mission report. Carbon ledger closing to 0.03 per cent; control run on the documented oxygen trajectory.

Accessibility notes

Both themes use tabular figures so numbers do not jitter as they change. Charts carry named units, safe operating bands and markers for your own interventions. Colour never carries meaning alone — every state is also carried by a label, a shape or a position.
