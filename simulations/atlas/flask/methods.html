01 The source

This is a simulation of the long-term evolution experiment (LTEE) begun by Richard Lenski in 1988: twelve populations of Escherichia coli, founded from a single ancestor, propagated in the same minimal glucose medium (DM25), transferred one part in a hundred every day, with a sample frozen every five hundred generations.

Historical position

The simulation is historically informed, not a reconstruction. Every screen labels its numbers as documented estimated invented or emergent, and the in-page Assumptions screen lists all four categories in full — including a section on where the model is deliberately wrong, and why.

The governing constraint

One rule shapes everything else:

Fitness is not reported. It is measured.

Nothing in the model decides who wins by comparing fitness numbers. Lineages grow on a shared and finite supply of carbon, and whoever ends the day with more cells has more cells at the next transfer. Relative fitness exists here only as the outcome of a competition assay you set up against a sample you were careful enough to freeze, paid for out of bench hours you could have spent on something else. The bench shows a dash where you have not looked.

02 Rules and equations

Growth is Monod kinetics on three carbon sources, integrated over each twenty-four hour cycle.

SourceConcentrationBehaviour in the model
Glucose25 µg/mL Exhausted about nine hours in. Most of a cell's life here is starvation.
Acetatesecreted Nobody adds it — it is excreted during growth on glucose and eaten afterwards.
Citrate500 µg/mL About twenty times the carbon of the glucose, and completely unavailable: the ancestor cannot bring it across the membrane in the presence of oxygen.

Where chance enters

At exactly two places, which is where it enters in the laboratory:

  1. Mutation. Mutations arise at random against a per-genome rate that can itself change when mismatch repair breaks.
  2. The daily transfer. One part in a hundred is a random sample of what was in the flask. This is where drift lives, and why a mutation arising late in the growth phase is usually lost no matter how good it is.

Every stochastic decision draws from a seeded Mulberry32 generator rather than Math.random, so same seed, same history holds exactly.

Emergent coexistence

Because the acetate available to a specialist depends on how many specialists there are, the model produces genuine negative frequency dependence. Stable two-lineage coexistence emerges without being written in anywhere — it is not a rule, it is a consequence. emergent

The genetic architecture

  • Seventeen heritable traits, each with a physiological range it cannot leave.
  • Twenty-six gene targets drawn from the real parallelism data.
  • Exponential beneficial effects with diminishing-returns epistasis.
  • Mismatch repair that can break, producing hypermutators.
  • A three-stage citrate innovation that requires a potentiated background before it is reachable at all.

03 Parameters

The bench-hour budget

One bench hour accrues per simulated day, to a ceiling of 120. You cannot measure everything, and that is the design rather than a limitation of it.

ProcedureCostWhat it tells you
Competition assay3 hRelative fitness against any frozen sample, three replicates, with error
Assay when rare5 hThe same competition started at 5 %, which is how you catch frequency dependence
Plate for colonies1 hCell size, density, and how many visibly different types there are
Sequence one clone8 hEvery mutation in one genome, drivers and passengers alike
Sequence the population18 hEvery lineage above five per cent
Reciprocal invasion6 hWhether two lineages exclude each other or coexist
Replay26 hTwenty restarts from one archived timepoint, two thousand generations each

Modes

  • The experiment as it was run — twelve populations, DM25, 37 °C, 1:100 daily transfer, frozen every 500 generations, target 50,000. The recommended first run.
  • Two thousand generations — the same design on a shorter clock, one sitting.
  • Design the experiment — a five-stage wizard covering medium, incubator, transfer regime, pressures and length, including the knobs that turn this into a different experiment entirely.
  • Blind bench — the historical protocol with half the bench hours, so that deciding what is worth knowing becomes the game.

The full constant set, gene table and mutation parameters live in js/config.js; the engine itself is js/model.js, which has no DOM access and runs headless under node. The code is the specification — this page is a summary of it.

04 What is simplified

Four things exist in the simulation that were no part of the original design. They are here because what if it had been run differently is worth being able to ask, and they are marked invented wherever they appear:

  • Phage
  • Antibiotics
  • Spatial structure
  • Alternating environments

The bench-hour budget is likewise a device rather than a feature of the real experiment.

Bounded by construction

Fitness is bounded because the traits are. Each has a physiological range it cannot leave, and a lineage sitting at the floor for its lag phase gets nothing from a mutation that would have shortened it. With every trait pushed to its limit at once, a Cit− lineage measures 2.14 and a Cit+ one 2.91. Nothing in the model can go beyond that.

05 What this cannot establish

  • It is not evidence about E. coli. A result here is a statement about this model, under its parameters, and nothing more.
  • It cannot settle whether the real citrate innovation was contingent. It reproduces the rate at which such an innovation appears under an assumed three-stage architecture — an architecture the model asserts rather than discovers.
  • It cannot extrapolate past its own ceilings. The trajectory flattens a little earlier than the real one after twenty thousand generations, because the trait bounds bind before the biology would.
  • It under-produces hypermutators: three to four of twelve by 50,000 generations against six of twelve in the real experiment. Any conclusion that turns on mutator frequency is unsafe.
  • It says nothing about mechanism at the molecular level. Gene targets are drawn from real parallelism data, but the model has no biochemistry underneath them.
