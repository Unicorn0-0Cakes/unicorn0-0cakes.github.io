01 The source

This is a model of the public-goods dilemma, the canonical form of the collective-action problem. Twenty-four people each receive private resources every year; their long-term survival depends on a shared system — the commons — that nobody is obliged to maintain.

Standing

The Commons is a model, not an experiment. It dramatises mechanisms described in cooperation research — public-goods dilemmas, conditional cooperation, reciprocity, reputation and altruistic punishment (Ostrom; Fehr & Gächter; Axelrod) — but its numbers are illustrative of those mechanisms, not measurements of any real population.

The tension

The pool is multiplied by the society's productivity and split equally. The group is best off if everyone contributes; each individual is personally best off keeping their share while others give. That is the whole game, and no rule you can set removes it.

Why private wealth cannot save anyone

A separate infrastructure stock represents the commons itself. It decays every year and is replenished only when average contribution rises above a maintenance line. Because production is damped when infrastructure is broken, a town full of rich hoarders still collapses.

02 Rules and equations

Agent-based, twenty-four agents, one decision per agent per year. You choose the length of the run: 4, 8, 20, 50, 100, 250, 500 or 1000 years.

The yearly loop

  1. Endowment — each living person receives resources scaled by abundance.
  2. Contribution — each decides how much to give, weighing dispositions, what they expect others to do, scarcity, resentment, the desire to be seen giving, and trust.
  3. Production — total contributions are multiplied by productivity, damped by how functional the infrastructure currently is.
  4. Distribution — the grown pool is shared equally; each keeps what they withheld.
  5. Update — reputations, trust, resentment and strategies shift. Exploited cooperators harden; free riders surrounded by a giving, punishing world soften.
  6. Reward / punishment — willing punishers pay a cost to sanction people they believe are free-riding, a belief that can be wrong. Visible top contributors may be rewarded.
  7. Shocks & upkeep — infrastructure decays; emergencies may strike.

The people

Agents are not fixed caricatures. Each is drawn with probabilities along ten dimensions:

conditional cooperation · self-preservation · fairness sensitivity · status desire willingness to punish · forgiveness · conformity · subgroup loyalty scarcity response · memory length

Behaviour then evolves. A cooperator repeatedly exploited becomes defensive; a free rider embedded in stable cooperation may start to contribute. Click any person to see how far they have drifted from where they started.

Belief is separable from truth

Punishment acts on beliefs about free-riding, and information accuracy is a control. A healthy society can collapse because people falsely believe others are cheating, punishing honest contributors until trust unravels; a fragile one can hold together simply because its records are trusted. The dashboard tracks false accusations as a first-class metric. emergent

04 What is simplified

  • Twenty-four people. Small enough that reputation can be global and everyone can in principle observe everyone. Real commons problems are usually larger and sparser.
  • One decision per year. There is no bargaining, no contract, no partial commitment and no timing — contribution is a single scalar chosen simultaneously.
  • The commons is one stock. Real shared systems are many overlapping resources with different renewal rates and different excludability.
  • Communication is a toggle, not a channel. When enabled it shifts expectations; no messages are actually exchanged, so nothing can be negotiated or lied about explicitly.
  • Ten dispositions, drawn once. Behaviour drifts, but the underlying dispositions do not; there is no learning model beneath them.
  • Exit is leaving, not choosing between commons. There is nowhere else to go.

05 What this cannot establish

  • It cannot demonstrate how any actual group must behave. It shows how a society built on certain rules could behave.
  • Its numbers are not measurements. A cooperation rate of 72 % in a run has no unit that maps onto anything observed.
  • It cannot rank real institutional designs. That punishment stabilises cooperation here is a property of the punishment rule as written, not evidence for sanctioning as policy.
  • It cannot separate the effect of a control from the effect of the archetype-detection rules that name the outcome. The Verdict is a classifier applied to the run, not a measurement of it.
  • It has no uncertainty quantification. Runs are single draws; the interface offers no batch mode, no seed control and no confidence interval on any reported quantity.

06 Validation status

Uncalibrated by design

No parameter in this model is fitted to empirical data. The mechanisms are taken from the cooperation literature; the magnitudes are chosen so that the mechanisms are legible on screen within a watchable number of years.

What was checked

  • Scenario reachability. Each of the six presets reliably produces the outcome it is named for, which is what makes them useful as controls against each other.
  • Mechanism isolation. The information-accuracy control moves the false-accusation metric and the collapse rate independently of abundance, confirming the belief channel is live rather than decorative.
  • Wealth cannot substitute for infrastructure. High private wealth with failed infrastructure still collapses, which is the property the production damping was written to have.

These are behavioural checks on the implementation, not validation against observation. Nothing here has been compared to a real public-goods experiment.
