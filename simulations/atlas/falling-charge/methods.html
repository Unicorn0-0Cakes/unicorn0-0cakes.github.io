01 What is being recreated

In 1913 Robert Millikan published measurements of the motion of charged oil droplets between two parallel plates, and argued from them that electric charge comes in a common indivisible unit. The apparatus cannot show a charge. It shows how fast a droplet falls, and how that changes when a field is applied. Everything else is inference.

The distinction that matters

Since the 2019 revision of the SI, the elementary charge is exact by definition: e = 1.602 176 634 × 10⁻¹⁹ C, and the ampere is defined in terms of it. No experiment measures the elementary charge any more. It is a fixed number, with zero uncertainty, and modern experiments measure other things against it.

This simulation recreates the inference, not the measurement of a modern constant. The exact SI value is used internally as the hidden ground truth that generates the droplets, and it is sealed until the user locks their analysis. The user is not redefining anything.

Historical position

The apparatus is period-informed, not a reconstruction. The plate spacing, chamber dimensions, oil density and observer reaction-time figures used in historical mode are plausible inventions. They have not been read from Millikan's paper. Historical mode is labelled period-inspired throughout for that reason, and the claim will not be upgraded until the primary text has been used as a source.

02 The physical model

Sign convention, fixed once and used everywhere. The vertical axis is positive upward, with its origin at the lower plate. V is the potential of the upper plate minus the lower, so E_y = −V/d: a positive voltage puts the field downward, and a positive droplet moves down. Oil-drop simulations usually go wrong here, so the canvas converts to screen coordinates in exactly one function.

The Cunningham slip correction

An 0.5 µm droplet is about eight mean free paths across, so gas molecules slip past its surface and the drag is less than the continuum law predicts. Ignoring this makes the inferred radius too large and the charge too large by roughly 11 %.

C_c = 1 + Kn·(α + β·exp(−γ/Kn)), with Kn = λ/r — the Knudsen number defined against the radius, not the diameter, because that is the convention the coefficients were fitted under, and mixing conventions produces a silent factor-of-two error.

Default coefficients α = 1.155, β = 0.471, γ = 0.596, from Allen and Raabe's least-squares re-evaluation of Millikan's own raw oil-drop data. Chosen because they were fitted to oil droplets in a Millikan apparatus, which is exactly the system being simulated. An alternative 1985 set and a no-correction option are both selectable.

Inverting the measurements

Radius, from the field-off fall speed. Because C_c depends on r the equation is implicit and is solved by bisection, with the iteration count and residual shown in the interface rather than hidden:

r²·C_c(r) = 9η·v_f / (2g(ρ_oil − ρ_air))

Charge, from the field-off fall and the field-on motion of the same droplet. One expression covers balanced suspension, slowed fall and terminal rise:

q = −6πηr·d·(v_f − v_s) / (C_c·V)

where v_s is the field-on velocity as a downward-positive speed — zero at balance, negative when rising. There is no code path from a voltage reading to a charge; the function throws unless both observations exist.

Integration

The velocity relaxation time is about 3 microseconds, so the system is extremely stiff and an explicit integrator would be useless. Because the drag is linear in velocity, the equation has a closed-form solution over each step, and that exact exponential update is what the model uses. It is unconditionally stable and exact for constant forces at any step size — which matters most near balance, the case that breaks naive schemes.

Physics advances in fixed 2 ms steps of simulated time. Frame rate cannot change the trajectory; this is asserted by running identical simulated time in chunks of 1, 7 and 331 steps and comparing bit for bit.

03 Inferring the unit

Given a set of measured charges, the task is to find the common unit they are multiples of, without dividing by a known answer. The analysis module imports nothing that contains the accepted value; the search range is derived from the data.

Why chi-squared alone cannot do this

Two problems, and only the second is usually noticed.

Exact ties. If e explains the data then e/2 explains it identically: every integer doubles and every residual is unchanged. Chi-squared cannot distinguish them at all.

Finer lattices genuinely fit better. Once measurement noise is comparable to e, a finer lattice has more rungs to absorb noise into, so χ²(e/3) is strictly lower than χ²(e). Minimising chi-squared drives the estimate toward zero. The first implementation of this simulation did exactly that and returned ê ≈ e/3.

The fix is a penalty for model flexibility, and it is derived rather than tuned. Writing the honest marginal likelihood of the quantised model — summing over the integer assignments instead of fitting them for free, with a uniform prior over the integers available in a data set spanning [0,Q] — gives

−2 ln L ≈ χ²(e) + 2N·ln(Q/e)

The penalty difference between e and e/2 is 2N ln 2, independent of the data, so more droplets make the ladder easier to see rather than harder. Both curves are plotted, so the user can see what the penalty did.

This is a load-bearing modelling choice. A different prior over integer states would give a different penalty and could move the estimate. A reviewer who disagrees should say so.

Implemented: Method A, the candidate-lattice search on the penalised objective, reporting the full curve, every local minimum including the sub-multiples, the integer assignments, ambiguous assignments, and the residuals. Method B, weighted regression through the origin using those assignments. Method B is not an independent confirmation of Method A — it consumes A's assignments — and the interface says so.

Not implemented: robust estimation, the likelihood mixture over integer states, and pairwise charge-step analysis.

04 Uncertainty

The correlated-residual correction, and what it broke

For a diffusing droplet the tracked positions are not independent — the noise is a random walk. Ordinary least squares assumes otherwise, and on simulated tracks its standard error understates the true velocity uncertainty by a factor of 13 to 50.

That was not a cosmetic error. With a tenfold overconfident velocity error, a neutral droplet passed a three-sigma "did it respond to the field?" test, entered the dataset carrying an apparent charge of a few hundredths of an elementary unit, and pulled the inferred lattice onto a sub-multiple. A bad uncertainty produced a wrong physical conclusion.

For Brownian motion the endpoints are a sufficient statistic for the drift, so the honest standard error is √(2D/T) — independent of how many samples were taken — with D estimated from the mean squared increment of the residuals. The instrument reports the larger of that and the OLS value, and shows both.

The same correlation wrecks the usual terminal-velocity check. On 3000 simulated tracks with no acceleration whatsoever, the median |t| on the quadratic coefficient is 8.0: the textbook |t| > 2 rule would reject 87 % of perfectly settled droplets. The instrument compares the size of the implied curvature against the observed noise instead, with a threshold set from that measured null distribution.

Random and systematic uncertainty are reported separately and never silently summed, because they behave differently: only the random part falls as more droplets are measured. A session can produce a beautiful integer ladder with reduced chi-squared of 1.0 and be 4 % wrong, because every droplet was measured with the same mis-scaled reticle. With a fixed systematic, interval coverage degrades as the sample grows — more data makes an uncorrected experiment more confidently wrong. That is the single most useful thing this instrument can demonstrate.

Propagation is Monte Carlo over the user's declared calibration uncertainties, because the radius inversion is implicit and analytic partials would require differentiating through a root-find. Sensitivity is computed by numerically perturbing the model, so the uncertainty budget reflects this model rather than a textbook ranking.

05 Safeguards

  • No hidden correction. Nothing is adjusted toward the accepted value.
  • No outcome-aware hints. The instrument never comments on whether an estimate is close to anything.
  • No disappearing observations. The storage layer has no delete operation for observations. Rejected measurements remain in the tables, the exports, the sensitivity analysis and the reveal.
  • No circular inference. The analysis module has no reference to the accepted value, no import of the constants file, and no access to the truth vault. Reading the vault before the reveal throws. A test strips comments and greps for violations — crude, and not a proof.
  • No false precision. Uncertainties are quoted to one significant figure (two if the leading digit is 1) and the estimate is rounded to match.
  • No erased protocol changes. Amendments preserve the previous rules, require a written reason, and record whether an intermediate estimate had already been seen.
  • Locking is irreversible. A locked analysis is deep-frozen; everything after the reveal is labelled outcome-aware exploratory analysis.

06 What is simplified

  • One transit per droplet. Millikan reversed the field repeatedly and averaged over many transits of the same droplet. This build derives a charge from one fall and one field-on observation, which is why per-measurement uncertainties sit at 5–10 % rather than under 1 %.
  • Uniform field. No edge or fringing effects near the plate boundary.
  • Dry air. Humidity affects both density and viscosity; not modelled.
  • No evaporation. Droplets carry the state field; nothing changes their radius.
  • No air currents. The quality indicator exists and is always false.
  • Tilt is half modelled. The velocity projection is applied; the lateral drift that would let a user notice the tilt is not rendered, which makes it an invisible systematic.
  • Local gravity is exact. Real local gravity varies by ±0.3 %, a 0.15 % systematic.
  • The mean free path is unsettled. Standard expressions give 66–67 nm; a 2023 molecular-dynamics study reports 38.5 nm. This model computes ≈65 nm from kinetic theory.
  • The visible sample is biased. Small droplets scatter less light, so the droplets a user can find are systematically larger than the droplets that exist. Realistic, disclosed at the reveal, and still a non-random sample.

Of the 40 registered parameters, 5 are exact SI definitions, 3 are sourced, 8 come from secondary summaries of primary papers, and 24 are marked not yet calibrated — invented modelling choices with no external justification. The apparatus error magnitudes and the entire droplet population model are in that last category.

07 What this cannot establish

Read this before quoting anything from it

Simulated data are evidence about this model, not about the physical world. No number this instrument produces is a measurement of anything.

  • Nothing about interval coverage. Coverage requires many replicated experiments. Batch mode is not built, so no claim about coverage is made anywhere.
  • Nothing about quantisation versus a continuous model. Model comparison is not implemented. It is deliberately withheld until it can be shown to fail to select quantisation when given continuously distributed synthetic charges — the risk that the comparison is rigged by construction is the most serious in the project, and shipping it untested would be worse than leaving it out.
  • Nothing about exclusion policy. The Analyst's Dilemma mode, which is the part that actually teaches the difference between defensible rejection and cherry-picking, is not built.
  • Nothing about how real users behave. Hypothesis H8 concerns people, not software, and would need a study with participants.
