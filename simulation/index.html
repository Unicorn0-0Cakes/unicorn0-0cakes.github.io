01 What this is

These are not demonstrations. A demonstration shows you a result; an instrument makes you go and get one. Every simulation here gives you a system, a set of controls, and instruments that are worse than the system — noisy, partial, expensive to use — because that is the situation the original experimenters were actually in.

The consequence is that you can be wrong in them. You can spend your whole measurement budget on the thing that turns out not to matter. You can lock in an analysis and then find out what the accepted value was. That is the point.

02 How to read an evidence badge

Every instrument carries exactly one. It says what kind of thing the model is, so that “this simulation shows…” can be checked rather than taken on trust. A new instrument renders a dashed status pending chip until its methods page can show the work — visibly unfinished beats quietly overclaiming.

Historical reconstruction
Rebuilds a documented experiment.
Established mathematical model
Published equations with known behaviour.
Calibrated research model
Tuned against published benchmarks; the table is shown.
Exploratory agent-based model
Dramatises a mechanism. The numbers illustrate; they do not measure.
Uncalibrated prototype
Internally consistent. Nothing has been fitted to data.
