The medium changes.
The systems thinking does not.

A bacterial population, a convolutional network, a crane company's homepage and a key photographed on a kitchen table are not obviously related. What they have in common is that each one is a system with a boundary, a set of things you can change, and a set of things you can only measure indirectly — and that the interesting work is almost always in the second set.

That shows up here as a habit. The simulations charge you bench hours for every measurement, so you have to decide what not to look for. The machine-learning write-ups report the class imbalance next to the accuracy. The research framework opens by saying it has trained nothing. None of that is modesty; it is the part of the work that makes the rest of it worth believing.
